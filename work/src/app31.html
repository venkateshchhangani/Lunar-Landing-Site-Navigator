How to read this tab, and what it cannot show
  • Match = our number is within the stated margin of the published number. Consistent = the published account gives no number, but it does not contradict ours (for example, a lander can only work if the Sun is above the horizon). Differs = outside the margin; it would be shown in red.
  • The margins were chosen when this tab was written, and each says why. A published value is only as good as its source. Apollo 11 comes from NASA's own mission report and the Apollo Lunar Surface Journal; Chandrayaan-3 and IM-2 come from Wikipedia and a news report, which are secondary sources.
  • This tab tests only the Sun, Earth and libration layer of the tool on a flat horizon (no terrain). No setting was adjusted for these missions: it is the same code and the same Sun and Earth positions as the other tabs.
  • On the left, only what the source publishes is moved to the published value (the Sun's height or the local time, say). Everything the source does not give (the Sun's direction around the horizon, where Earth is) is drawn as we calculate it. Where a source only says "the Sun was up", the left picture shows the lowest the Sun could have been for that to be true: on the horizon.
  • The pictures are not to scale (the Moon, Earth and Sun are shrunk and moved closer so they fit on one screen). Their directions, the Earth's real turn on that date and the Moon's lighting are real. Earth is the NASA Blue Marble picture, the Moon is the NASA LRO colour map, and the stars and the Milky Way band are drawn by code, not copied from any picture.
  • Sun times use the upper edge of the Sun against a flat horizon. A real site with hills or a crater rim gets its Sun later and loses it earlier (IM-2 landed in a shadowed crater). So a "Match" on durations means the geometry agrees, not that the ground does.
  • Known simplification: the page treats Earth as seen from the Moon's centre. Compared with an exact calculation made offline (IAU Moon rotation, with parallax), Earth heights differ by 0.13°, 0.22° and 0.28° at these three landings, and Sun heights by 0.02° or less. This is below every margin used here.
  • We do not know how these teams planned their dates. We only show that our geometry reproduces what they published about the result.
